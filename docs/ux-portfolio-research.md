# UX/UI Portfolio Website Research — April 2026

## Top Trends for UX Portfolios in 2026

### 1. "Less is More" Minimalism
- Generous white space, clean typography, subtle animations
- Avoid long eloquent intros — be concise and direct
- Dominant colors with sharp accents outperform timid, evenly-distributed palettes

### 2. Strong Case Study Storytelling
- Hiring managers compare multiple case studies side by side
- They look for clarity, reasoning quality, and ability to explain decisions under constraints
- Show: problem framing → research → wireframes → iterations → measurable results
- Simon Pan's portfolio is the gold standard — detailed case studies with Uber and Amazon showing impact

### 3. AI Integration & Proficiency
- Standout portfolios showcase how you use AI as a creative partner
- A modern portfolio should clearly outline your AI stack and how it speeds up your work
- AI chatbot assistants that answer questions about a designer's work, skills, and case studies are emerging

### 4. Interactive & Immersive Experiences
- Hover effects, animated transitions, clickable prototypes
- Some designers introduce interactive elements that integrate design work
- Focus on high-impact moments: one well-orchestrated page load with staggered reveals creates more delight than scattered micro-interactions

### 5. Emphasis on Process and Results
- Hiring managers want to see:
  - How you frame complex problems
  - How you use AI as a creative partner without losing your judgment
  - How you tie design decisions to business outcomes
  - How you help teams move forward when things are changing fast

### 6. Code Proficiency
- If you spend time working directly in code, your portfolio should make it clear
- Comfortable with IDEs like VS Code, can quickly prototype and refine in code
- This is a strong differentiator for UX designers

## What Makes Outstanding Portfolios Stand Out

### Key Design Principles
- Platform choice matters far less than content quality
- One primary purpose: demonstrate your ability to think like a UX designer
- Tell the story of how you got from point A to point B using compelling storytelling
- Include examples spanning the UX design process — research insights, concept sketches, wireframes, prototypes

### Content Over Flash
- Hiring managers are looking at your UX process and problem-solving, not whether you hand-coded your site
- But showing both (great process + hand-coded site) is a strong signal
- Portfolios that include measurable impact (metrics, before/after) are significantly more compelling

## Notable Portfolio Examples

### Simon Pan
- Gold standard for what hiring managers look for
- Detailed case studies including Uber Magic 2.0 and Amazon Prime Music
- Demonstrates sophisticated process documentation and measurable impact

### Niklas Bubori
- Award-winning UX designer from Switzerland
- Combines smooth animations and interactive design elements with clean navigation
- Creates natural flow through detailed case studies

### Lu Yu
- Interaction designer, Head of Brand at Pitch, Awwwards & Digital Design award jury member
- Great consistent colors, fantastic transitions and microinteractions
- Distinctive typography and well-used negative space

### Quentin Dallaserra
- Blends artistic flair with technical expertise
- Mastery of modern web design principles
- Each project demonstrates strong narrative and visual cohesion

### Irene Butenko
- Puts award-winning work front and center
- Clean presentation lets the work speak

### Aarron Walter
- Fourth hire at Mailchimp (Director of UX), VP of Design Education at InVision
- Simple yet effective personal site that showcases expertise without overdesigning

### Karolis Kosas
- Minimalist black-and-white approach
- Comprehensive case studies presented through unadorned grids with clear visual representations

### Mia
- Clean, modern, easy to navigate
- Case studies communicate problem space and design value
- Demonstrates thoughtful UX process and confident visual execution
